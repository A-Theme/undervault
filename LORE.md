# The Lore of the Undervault

*A guide to Emberfall, the Lantern Order and the dark beneath the city.*

</div>

> *Emberfall was built on a lid.*

Everything here comes from the game itself: room descriptions, boss dialogue, the fifteen lore scrolls and the endings. The first half is safe to read before you play. **Spoilers are folded away** further down; open them when you're ready.

---

## Emberfall

Three hundred years ago the **Lantern Order** chained the **Ashen Sovereign** beneath the city, a king who tried to burn the dark out of the world and nearly burned the world instead. Five **Wardstones** hold the seal, one beneath another, each kept burning by an oath and a keeper.

Above them, in the Temple, hangs the **Last Lantern**, the great lamp that mirrors the Wardstones below. Tonight it guttered and turned blue. One by one, the Wardstones are going dark.

Sister Maren, the last of the Order, sent for anyone who can hold a weapon. You came. You're not sure why you were the one who answered. *It felt like being called by name.*

### The people of Emberfall

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sister Maren** | Last of the Lantern Order. *"Younger than her voice,"* robes scorched at the hem. She ties the thread of blue light around your wrist that pulls you home when you fall. |
| **Brakka Ironjaw** | The smith, *"broad as a door."* Her racks are sorted by the depth of the dark each blade was made to cut. *"Go deeper and I forge deeper."* |
| **Old Tobble** | Keeps the Menagerie, *"mostly beard and pockets, and several of the pockets are moving."* He tends the beasts that come back from below. |
| **Wenna** | Runs the Guttered Candle, a smoky inn *"full of people who do not ask what you do for work."* |
| **Pell** | Guards the Undervault Gate. *"Down's down. The thread'll pull you back if it goes bad. Mostly."* |
| **Quillon the Peddler** | Turns up on the floors below with sacks of goods and too many teeth. *"Everything's for sale, friend. Even the things that aren't mine."* |

### Places in town

- **The Temple of the Last Lantern**: candles line every wall, *"left by the families of keepers who went down and did not come up."*
- **Emberfall Square**: a dry fountain carved with five lanterns. Someone has chalked across it: **WHO FEEDS THE LIGHT?**
- **The Undervault Gate**: a circle of black iron, warm to the touch. Old claw marks score the inside of the grate, *"every one of them made from below."*

## The descent: five Wardstones

| | Act | What waits below |
|---|---|---|
| I | **The Drowned Cellars** | The cisterns flooded the night the first Wardstone cracked. The water came up black and tasting of ash, and the Warden who kept it *went down to drink it so the city would not have to.* |
| II | **The Mycelial Deep** | The stone gives way to living rot. The Order's gardener, Sister Ilse, came down to *grow a seal out of herself.* The fungus sings now, in a voice like many sleeping children. |
| III | **The Clockwork Ossuary** | The Order buried its dead in machines, so even their bones could keep watch. *They no longer remember for whom.* |
| IV | **The Shattered Library** | Every oath the Order ever swore, in a library that grows downward like a root. Someone has been unwriting them, and the further you go, *the more pages carry your family's name.* |
| V | **The Ember Throne** | At the bottom of the world a crowned shape sits on a throne of cooling slag. He has been waiting three hundred years, *and he has been waiting for you specifically.* |

### The keepers

- **Grisholt, the Bloated Warden**: *"Another one come to take my water? It's MINE. I drink it so the wells stay clean. I drink it ALL."*
- **Mother Mycelia**: *"Have you come to be planted? The seal is so hungry, and I am so tired of being its garden."*
- **The Bone Engine**: the skeletons of the Order's founding knights, fused into one ticking colossus. *"INTRUDER. LINEAGE UNKNOWN. INITIATING... REMEMBRANCE."*
- **Archivist Venn, the Unwritten**: robes made of pages, a face that is a blank page. *"Ah. The last Vale."*
- **The Ashen Sovereign**: a crown that is not worn but burning. When he looks at you, you know his face. *It is a little like your own.*

## Side passages

Pell can take you down three older, stranger tunnels once the right Wardstone burns again.

- **The Smugglers' Warren**: tunnels under the docks, dug by Quillon's cousins (*"not the friendly side of the family"*). It answers to **Mag Ratchet**, *who has never once paid a toll.*
- **The Rime Vaults**: the Order's ice-house, where relics and the honoured dead were kept from rotting. **Abbess Hild** kept the vaults for sixty years. *She is keeping them still.*
- **The Moth Market**: below the Library, where unwritten words drift down and settle, ghosts trade in memories, names and the warmth of old summers. **The Auctioneer** *has had his eye on you since you were born.*

## The Abyss

Beneath the Ember Throne the world simply stops. Whatever lies behind that door has no shape and no bottom, only hunger, and *it has noticed that you are made of light.* Every fifth floor, a **Herald of the Hunger** waits, *"wearing the faces of every keeper the seal ever swallowed."* How deep you go is posted to the [online leaderboard](README.md#the-abyss-and-the-leaderboard).

## Book III: Across the Ashen Sea

After the Hunger, a ship comes to Emberfall, the first in years: the **Dawnwake**, out of **Solenne**, her sails the colour of candle flame. Three years ago every lantern in Solenne began to burn violet. The sea went grey. People began to vanish, down into the dark beneath the city. Its scholars say the thing beneath Emberfall had more than one door, and that one of them is theirs.

| Who | |
|---|---|
| **Queen Aurelie** | Young, ink on her fingers, a plain silver circlet pushed back in her hair. She stands when you come in, which queens are not supposed to do. |
| **Captain Ines Varo** | Captain of the Dawnwake. *"Stay in the lane and I'll get you there. Make me leave it and I make no promises."* |
| **Teodora Brisk** | The wayfinder at the North Gate. *"The road remembers people. The Greywold doesn't."* |
| **Magister Oriel Quell** | Of Solenne's Glass College, with a gate of brass and green glass at the Harbour Light. The chance it puts you somewhere else is *"very small. Smallish."* |
| **Archivist Sabine Quell** | Oriel's sister, at the Glass College, with none of her patience. |
| **Odo Fairweather** | Master of Hammerhall, where the smiths sing in teams of six. |
| **Old Wick** | Last guild-master of Solenne's lamplighters. *"It's not the lamps, delver. It's what they're burning."* |
| **Bettany** | Keeps the Salt Lamp, the only lantern in Solenne still burning gold. She talks to it. |

**The crossing.** By sea: the Ember Coast, the Ashen Sea, the Drowned Reach and the Glass Shoals, where the **Lamplit Widow** dangles a lantern like the ones from home. On foot: the North Road, the Greywold, the Teeth, the Hollow Marches and the Sunward Road, where the **Antlered King** keeps the road, and takes everything as his toll.

**The Underside.** A wound in every place, held open by the **Anchor**, a knot of torn sky in chains. You fall up into it. The thread does not reach it. Its relics: the Inverted Crown, the Eye of the Anchor, a Splinter of Torn Sky, the Ring of Falling Up, the Unknotted Band, and Your Reflection's Ring.

**The Drowned Stair** goes down beneath Solenne, flooded, lit violet from somewhere very deep, and barred by the Queen's guard. For now.

## Twelve who answered

Warrior, Mage, Rogue, Cleric, Ranger and Necromancer were the first. Now the **Druid** (who speaks for the wild), the **Brawler** (who brings their fists), the **Demon** (half-born of the Hunger itself), the **Scion** (heir to a fallen house of the Order), the **Elementalist** and the **Worg** answer Maren's call too. Whoever you are, the dark will learn your name, and give you a title for it.

Behind the Palace of Dawn a stair goes down under the sea, and the sea makes room for it. For three years the people of Solenne have been walking down it in their sleep.

At the end there is a choice: close the second door, or turn the tide. Either way, Arden Vale left a chart by the door: seven doors, in seven kingdoms. Two are shut now. *The story continues.*

## Book IV: The Lantern War

After the second door, a rider comes down the North Road under a banner of cold iron. In Emberfall's Square, standing on the rim of the dry fountain while frost forms under her boots, **Lady Corva Thane** reads out the **Ember Writ**: a charter three hundred years old, by which a king of Kaldmark gave a knight called Arden Vale his lantern. *What the crown gives, the crown may ask for again.* **King Osric of Kaldmark** claims the city, the Last Lantern and the Vale blood. His sappers are already in the cellars.

The war ends with a choice. Then the summers have to come home, and somebody has to go and ask for them, in a garden at the top of the world, from **Lily the Sweethang**: a lady in a wide sunhat who loves warm weather more than anything in this world, and who did not come from it.

## Raids

Some things below Emberfall are too big for one lantern: **the Choir Below**, every keeper the water ever took, singing with one voice; **the Founders' Colossus**, the Order's first machine; and, under the Abyss, **the Maw** of the Hunger itself. They take a party of friends.

### Book I: The Last Lantern

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter I: The Warden's Water</b> · The Drowned Cellars, floors 1-4</summary>

The first Wardstone lies in the Drowned Cellars, under the city's old cisterns. Its keeper, Warden Grisholt, went down to guard it thirty years ago. The water came up black the night the stone cracked. Find the Warden. Relight the stone.

**Its keeper: Grisholt, the Bloated Warden.**

- **What the Warden knew.** Grisholt drank the poisoned water for thirty years so nobody else would have to. The stone wanted a lantern a month, Maren told him, and she would not say what was in the lanterns. So he sent the children who carried them back up. Why would anyone need sending back?
- **What comes next.** The first Wardstone burns, and far above, the Last Lantern brightens. Tell Maren, and see what she says about the Warden. Then the Mycelial Deep, below the cisterns, where Sister Ilse keeps the second stone.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter II: The Gardener's Children</b> · The Mycelial Deep, floors 5-8</summary>

Below the cisterns lies the Mycelial Deep, a garden gone to rot. Sister Ilse, the Order's gardener, went down to grow a seal out of herself when the second stone began to fail. And someone has started chalking a question on the fountain: WHO FEEDS THE LIGHT?

**Its keeper: Mother Mycelia.**

- **What the gardener knew.** Mother Mycelia was Ilse, the Order's gardener. She grew the seal out of her own body so the stone would eat her instead of the children, and it hurt her for years. Her last words: the seal eats, child. Ask Maren what she feeds it.
- **What comes next.** Two stones burn. Tell Maren, and watch her face when you say Ilse's name. Below lies the Clockwork Ossuary, where the Order's founding knights still keep watch in brass and bone.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter III: Lineage</b> · The Clockwork Ossuary, floors 9-12</summary>

The Order buried its founding knights in the Clockwork Ossuary, fused into machines so their bones could keep watch. Somewhere among them is the tomb of the Order's first commander, Arden Vale, and a machine that keeps a record of every keeper there ever was.

**Its keeper: The Bone Engine.**

- **What the bones knew.** The founding knights, fused into one machine, still checked the blood of everyone who came. It checked yours: DESCENDANT OF ARDEN VALE CONFIRMED. WELCOME HOME, VALE. You are of the family the whole Order was built around.
- **What comes next.** Three stones burn. Maren knows what Vale means, if she will say it. Below lies the Shattered Library, where every oath the Order swore was written down, and someone has been unwriting them.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter IV: The Unwritten</b> · The Shattered Library, floors 13-16</summary>

Every oath the Order swore was written in the Shattered Library, and someone has been unwriting them. Archivist Venn is down there among the drifting pages. So are the Temple's orphan rolls, and the answer to where the orphans go.

**Its keeper: Archivist Venn, the Unwritten.**

- **What the archivist knew.** The seal was never a prison. It is a mouth. Every keeper fed to it was a Vale by blood or by adoption, and Maren adopts a great many orphans. Venn unwrote the Order's oaths so that no one else would have to die for it.
- **What comes next.** Four stones burn. Maren is waiting, and she knows what you know now. Below the Library lies the Ember Throne, and the king the Order chained there three hundred years ago. The last Wardstone takes a Vale.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter V: The Crown</b> · The Ember Throne, floors 17-20</summary>

At the bottom of the world, on a throne of black glass, waits the Ashen Sovereign, the king the Order chained three hundred years ago. A third oath was cut from the Order's charter. Find what is left of it. Every lore scroll you carry is part of the answer.

**Its keeper: The Ashen Sovereign.**

- **What the king knew.** The Ashen Sovereign is Caius Vale, Arden's brother, and your cousin. Beneath the world is the Hunger, which eats light, names and memory. Arden sealed Caius below to hold its door shut, and the Order fed the seal with Vales to keep him strong. For three hundred years.
- **The choice.** Relight the seal with your blood and become its keeper, as Caius was. Take the crown and hold the Hunger back alone, as a king. Or, if you carry all fifteen scrolls, speak the third oath the Order cut from every book: to share the burden, so no one carries it alone.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How Book I can end</b></summary>

- You relit the seal with your own blood and became its keeper, as Caius had been.
- You took his burning crown, to hold the Hunger back yourself, alone, as a king.
- You spoke the third oath with him and broke the crown in two, so no one would carry it alone.

</details>

### Book II: The Hunger Below

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter VI: Your Name in the Dark</b> · The Unlit Stair, floors 21-24</summary>

Beneath the Ember Throne, where the histories end, a stair goes down. Everything the Hunger has ever taken went down it: light, and names, and the people who carried them. Keep saying your own name. Something down there is learning it.

**Its keeper: The Herald of Names.**

- **The names go home.** The Herald wore a thousand names the Hunger had stolen. When it fell they flew back up the stair to whoever they belonged to, and one of them was yours. Whatever is below has been learning you.
- **What comes next.** Tell Maren what you saw on the stair. Below lies the Hall of the Fed, where the Hunger keeps what it has eaten.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter VII: The Ones Who Were Given</b> · The Hall of the Fed, floors 25-28</summary>

The Hunger does not let go of what it eats. It keeps it. Below the stair lies the hall where it keeps three hundred years of the Order's orphans, the children given to the seal. They are still waiting for someone to take them home.

**Its keeper: Aster Vale, the First Fed.**

- **The First Fed.** Aster Vale was six when the Order gave her to the seal at its making, so it would know its keeper's blood. She was Caius's daughter. For three hundred years she grew vast and hungry in the dark, waiting for her father. Now she is asleep.
- **What comes next.** Tell Maren. Below the hall lies the Archive of Ash, where the Hunger leaves what it has made the world forget.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter VIII: What the Hunger Forgot</b> · The Archive of Ash, floors 29-32</summary>

The Hunger eats memory the way fire eats paper, and it leaves ash. In the Archive of Ash lie the Order's lost records, and Tick, the old recording engine, copying everything twice. Something down there does not want them read.

**Its keeper: The Unwriter.**

- **The ash remembers.** The Unwriter only ever took. When it fell, every page it had eaten came loose at once: birthdays, faces, voices, and the third oath, which went up the stair as fast as it could. The Hunger cannot eat what is written twice.
- **What comes next.** Tell Maren what Caius asked of Arden at the door. Below the archive a wall runs off into the dark, and someone has been keeping watch on it for a very long time.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter IX: One More Day</b> · Arden's Watch, floors 33-36</summary>

The histories say Arden Vale founded the Lantern Order. They are wrong. Arden went down, through the door, and built a watch inside the Hunger itself. He has stood on its walls ever since. It has been a long time since his light had a name.

**Its keeper: Arden, Hollowed.**

- **The watch is kept.** Arden Vale did not found the Order. He went down to hold the door from the far side, alone, one more day at a time, for three hundred years, until the watch was all that was left of him. You gave him his name back. He gave you his lantern.
- **What comes next.** Tell Maren. Then go down to the Heart of the Hunger, with Arden's light and every name you carry.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter X: The Hunger Below</b> · The Heart of the Hunger, floors 37-40</summary>

At the bottom there is no bottom: only the Hunger, wearing everything it has ever taken. It has no name, because names are a kind of light. The fifteen scrolls of the deep hold a way to end this that nobody has ever tried.

**Its keeper: The Hunger Below.**

- **The Hunger Below.** It is not dead. Things like this do not die. But for the first time it is waiting to see what someone will do. It was not always hungry. Once, before the world, it was only empty, and emptiness can be filled.
- **The choice.** Give it a name, if you carry all fifteen scrolls of the deep: names are a kind of light, and a thing with a name can be full. Or set Arden's light in its door, close it from outside, and keep the watch, so that no one need be fed.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How Book II can end</b></summary>

- You gave it a name, and for the first time since before the world it was full. It let go of the light, the names and the children.
- You closed its door with Arden's light and set a watch on it, so no one need ever be fed to it again.

</details>

### Book III: Across the Ashen Sea

<details>
<summary><b>The Dawnwake, and Solenne</b></summary>

Now a ship has come, the first in years: the Dawnwake, out of Solenne, a kingdom across the Ashen Sea whose lanterns burn violet. Her captain carries a letter from a queen, and the letter asks for you by name.

- **Solenne.** A kingdom across the Ashen Sea: white towers on grey cliffs, a harbour full of ships that no longer sail, a Glass College of scholars, and a young queen, Aurelie, who sent a ship to find you.
- **The violet lamps.** Three years ago every lantern in Solenne began to burn violet. Then the sea went grey. Then people began to vanish: fishermen, lamplighters, children, walking down into the sea in their sleep. The old Queen, Ysmay, went down after them and never came back.
- **Why you.** Solenne's scholars say the thing under Emberfall was one mouth of something larger, and that it had more than one door. One of them is here. Present yourself to the Queen at the Palace of Dawn, north of Sunfall Square.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XII: The Drowned Stair</b> · The Drowned Stair, floors 41-44</summary>

Behind the Palace of Dawn a stair goes down under the sea, and the sea makes room for it. The sleepwalkers walk down it. Nobody walks up. The Glass College has tuned your thread to Solenne: below here, it brings you up to the College, not home.

**Its keeper: Ondine, the Tide-Warden.**

- **The stair is open.** Ondine the Tide-Warden was Ondine Marsh, an apprentice lamplighter, drowned and made into the stair's guard. The sleepers walk down because something below calls them, and the lamps are part of it.
- **What comes next.** Tell the Queen. Below the Gate of Shells lie the lamplighters' lost works, and the violet light grows brighter.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XIII: The Violet Lamps</b> · The Violet Lamps, floors 45-48</summary>

Solenne's lamps were fed from below, by the lamplighters' guild works: brass pipes and clean gold oil from the salt seams. Three years ago the oil turned violet. The guild master, Aurel Fenn, went down to see. Old Wick has lit the lamps by hand and waited for him ever since.

**Its keeper: Aurel Fenn, the Lampwright.**

- **The lamps go gold.** The violet light was never oil. It is the light of people the sea took, pumped up through the guild's pipes to burn in Solenne's lamps. Aurel Fenn made it brighter, and it burned him hollow. With the works quiet, a few lamps above burn gold again.
- **What comes next.** Tell the Queen, and Old Wick. Below the works lies the drowned city of the first kings, and the bargain that started all of this.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XIV: The Sunken Court</b> · The Sunken Court, floors 49-52</summary>

Solenne was not always where it stands. Nine hundred years ago the sea took the old city, and King Solen led the survivors up the cliff. The history books leave out what he did first. He went down, and made a bargain, and the city has been paying it ever since.

**Its keeper: Solen the First, the Drowned King.**

- **The treaty breaks.** King Solen bought his city back from the sea for one light a year, then a month, then a day, then whatever it asked. Every king after him paid. He only ever meant to buy one more day.
- **What comes next.** Tell the Queen. Below the court the current pulls harder, as if it felt the treaty break, and somewhere at the bottom of it the old Queen is holding.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XV: The Undertow</b> · The Undertow, floors 53-56</summary>

Below the court the sea turns into a current, and the current pulls everything the sea has ever taken down toward something thirsty. Three years ago a queen went down to hold it back with her own light. She may still be holding.

**Its keeper: Queen Ysmay, Keeper of the Tide.**

- **The keeper lets go.** Queen Ysmay held the Undertow back with her own light for three years, so it would not take the city. When she let go, she told you what she had learned: it was never the lamps. It was the door.
- **What comes next.** Take her lantern to Aurelie. Then go down the current, faster now, toward a door you can feel from here: open, and thirsty.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XVI: The Second Door</b> · The Second Door, floors 57-60</summary>

The Hunger had more than one door. Under Emberfall the door was shut from inside. Under Solenne it was opened from outside, nine hundred years ago, and it has stood open ever since. Everything the sea takes goes through it. The fifteen scrolls of the drowned hold a way to turn the tide.

**Its keeper: The Undertow, the Second Mouth.**

- **The second mouth.** The Undertow is the Hunger's cousin: not hungry but thirsty, drinking everything the tide has ever taken through a door a king opened nine hundred years ago. It is not dead. The drowned lanterns are watching to see what you will do.
- **The choice.** Close the second door with Ysmay's lantern and keep the watch, as Arden did. Or, with all fifteen scrolls of the drowned, turn the tide: give back what it holds, every drowned light, and let it be only a tide again.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How Book III can end</b></summary>

- You closed the second door with Queen Ysmay's lantern and set a watch on it, as Arden did on the first.
- You turned the tide. Every drowned light went back up the current, and the Undertow became only a tide again.

</details>

*Arden's chart shows seven doors, in seven kingdoms. Two are shut now. Five are not, and something stands behind each one, listening. Queen Aurelie is looking for ships. Until they are ready, the deep places, their Nightmares and the Abyss are all still there.*

### Book IV: The Lantern War

<details>
<summary><b>The Ember Writ</b></summary>

King Osric of Kaldmark claims Emberfall, the Last Lantern and the Vale blood, by a charter three hundred years old: the Ember Writ, which gave Arden Vale his lantern. His sappers are already in the cellars. Hale is setting up a war table in the Watchtower.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XVII: The Ember Writ</b> · The Sappers' Tunnels, floors 61-64</summary>

Kaldmark's sappers came up through the Smugglers' Warren and are digging toward the wells. Somebody sold them the way in. Find their command post, find the turncoat, and stop Sapper-General Brune before she reaches the cisterns.

**Its keeper: Sapper-General Brune.**

- **What the general knew.** Brune's sappers were not cruel, only cold. Someone in the Warren sold them the tunnels for warm coats and salt pork. The king, she said, is not a bad man. He is a cold one.
- **What comes next.** Report to the war table. Kaldmark is coming out of the ground: the siege of the Gate has begun, and the iron king has built something to throw at the Lantern.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XVIII: Hold the Gate</b> · The Siege of the Gate, floors 65-68</summary>

Kaldmark is out of the ground and the siege has begun. Emberfall holds the Square, the Temple and the Gate behind barricades of carts and pews. The iron king has built an engine to put a stone through the Last Lantern. Hold the Gate.

**Its keeper: The Breaker.**

- **What the engineer knew.** Marta Vey had never missed. The Breaker threw ice because there is nothing left in Kaldmark that burns. When it fell, men all along the lines looked at it, and then north, and then at each other.
- **What comes next.** Report to the war table. Kaldmark is falling back to the Greywold, and Solenne's ships are coming up the coast.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XIX: Ships on the Horizon</b> · The Greywold Front, floors 69-72</summary>

Kaldmark has pulled back north to the Greywold, and the war is in the open. Solenne's fleet is waiting off the coast for a signal. On a hill across the grass, the Frost Marshal is waiting for you, white with the cold of the third door.

**Its keeper: The Frost Marshal.**

- **What the Marshal knew.** Isolde Rhyne went to the third door thirty years ago, and the cold went into her. The king opened the door to get warm: it gave Kaldmark one summer, and then asked for all of them. She was warm at the end. She wanted the king to know.
- **What comes next.** Report to the war table. Then over the Teeth, into Kaldmark, to see what Osric has been fighting for.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XX: The Cold Villages</b> · Behind the Teeth, floors 73-76</summary>

Over the Teeth is Kaldmark, and it has been winter there for fifty years. Red lamps take the warmth out of every hearth and send it north to the king, to feed the door under his throne. Go and see what Osric has been fighting for.

**Its keeper: The Rust-Bishop.**

- **What the bishop knew.** The red lamps took the warmth of every hearth in Kaldmark and fed it to the door under the king's throne, a little from everyone, so the rest could live. But the door does not want warmth. It wants to be warm. Nothing has ever shared with it.
- **What comes next.** Report to the war table. Then Hollowcrown, and the king, and the third door.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XXI: The Iron Claimant</b> · Hollowcrown, floors 77-80</summary>

At the top of Kaldmark's last mountain sits Hollowcrown, and on its iron throne sits King Osric, who opened the third door to get warm and has been feeding it ever since. The war ends there, one way or another.

**Its keeper: King Osric of Kaldmark, the Iron Claimant.**

- **What the king knew.** Osric opened the third door to get warm, and the cold behind it has asked for more ever since. He fed it his kingdom's hearths, and then wanted Emberfall's Lantern, because he could not think of anything else. He never read the third oath. His family folded it under for three hundred years.
- **The choice.** Take his iron crown and end the war by conquest. Go down alone with Emberfall's light and shut his door from outside. Or, with all fifteen war scrolls, take him with you and share the light.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How the war can end</b></summary>

- You took King Osric's iron crown and ended the war by conquest. Kaldmark knelt, and stayed cold.
- You went down alone with Emberfall's light and shut the third door from outside. Kaldmark lived, cold and polite.
- You and King Osric shared the light at the third door, Emberfall's and Kaldmark's last coal, and the door was warm for the first time, and closed by itself.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Chapter XXII: Everlasting Summer</b> · the garden at the top of the world</summary>

South of anywhere anyone has drawn, on the last warm mountain, there is a garden where it is always summer. A lady in a sunhat keeps it, and everything in it was paid for by somebody else. Her name is Lily.

**Its keeper: Lily the Sweethang.**

- Lily kneels in her garden, and the light goes out of her wings, and for the first time you see what is under all that summer: someone very far from home, and very, very cold.
- "It was only ever... so cold here," she says. "I just wanted it to be summer for everyone." She opens her hands, and the summers she has kept for fifty years rise out of the garden like birds, all of them at once, and fly north.

You faced Lily the Sweethang in her garden at the top of the world, and she let the summers go. They flew north like birds, and every village in Kaldmark woke up warm.

</details>

*Arden's chart shows seven doors. Three are shut now. One of the four that are not is in the sky, and something fell out of it once. Lily's garden is already growing back, and she is waiting for you, and your friends.*

<summary><b>The truth about the seal</b></summary>

The Ashen Sovereign is **Caius Vale**, and you are his blood. Caius never tried to burn the world. He went down to fight the thing beneath it: **the Hunger Below**, which eats light, names and memory. His brother **Arden** sealed him in to hold the door shut.

The Lantern Order kept the seal fed so Caius would stay strong enough to hold it, and what it fed the seal was the Vale line, by blood or by adoption. For thirty years Sister Maren has sent orphans down through the Gate and told herself the light was worth it. *"I have been sorry every day."*

There were three oaths: **to seal, to rule, and to share the burden.** The Order struck the third from every book.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>The truth beneath the Throne (Book II)</b></summary>

The Hunger keeps what it eats. The orphans the Order fed to the seal are still down there, in a hall of small beds and burned-out candles. The first of them was **Aster Vale**, aged six: Caius's daughter, given to the seal at its making so that it would know its keeper's blood.

**Arden** did not go home and found the Order. He went down after sealing his brother, built a watch on the far side of the door, and held it alone for three hundred years: *one more day.* The Order kept the first oath, feared the second, and burned the third, *because a burden that is shared cannot be sold.*

The Hunger has no name, because names are a kind of light. It was not always hungry: once it was only empty, and emptiness can be filled.

- **The Doorwarden**: you set Arden's light in the door at the Heart and close it from outside. The Hunger sleeps, the watch is kept, and no one need be fed.
- **The Namegiver** (with all fifteen scrolls of the deep): you give the Hunger a name. A self can be full; for the first time it lets go, and the children go home up the stair like lanterns on a river.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>The three endings</b></summary>

- **The Keeper's Light** (relight the seal): you give your blood to the last Wardstone and become the keeper. Caius rests for the first time in three hundred years, and Maren lights a lantern for you in the Temple window every night after.
- **The Ashen Heir** (take the crown): you hold the Hunger alone, a king of ash on a throne of glass. No more Vales are fed to the dark. The city doesn't know why it feels so safe, or so cold.
- **The Third Oath** (the true ending, with all fifteen scrolls): you and Caius break the crown in half together. When the Hunger stirs again there will be many lanterns and many hands, and *no one will be fed to the dark.* Maren is waiting at the Gate, crying and smiling.

</details>
